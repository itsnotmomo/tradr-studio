# Tradr News — Media Playbook

_Last updated: 13 Nov 2026 (§11 added the same evening). Built from a study of @polymarket's Instagram (788K followers, 1,863 posts at the time of the study): **~191 posts read** (metadata, caption, likes/comments/shares, slide count), **16 carousels stepped through slide by slide**, date range **11 May 2026 → 13 Nov 2026** (every post from 24 Oct to 13 Nov, then every 3rd, then every 6th post going back). Raw log and reference screenshots: `.news/pm-study/`._

This is decision-making guidance for whoever edits Tradr News, not a template. Where it says "Polymarket does X", that is evidence; where it says "we", that is the recommendation. `docs/news-style-guide.md` holds the production rules (fonts, sizes, Studio fields); where this playbook's evidence differs from it, the difference is called out in "Adapting to Tradr" for Mohamed to decide.

---

## 1. Direction & tone

- **One template, many stories.** Every editorial post is the same object: a 1080×1350 carousel, photo hook with a short all-caps headline, then white "text above media" slides. In 480 grid posts only 5 were reels. The consistency *is* the brand; the account is recognisable in the feed without reading the name.
- **Newsroom facts, internet reactions.** The facts (slide text and caption) read like a wire service: "Reuters reported Thursday", "according to the Wall Street Journal". The carousel's *texture* is internet-native: memes, X posts, TV clips. Roughly 35–45% of inner slides in the carousels I stepped through are memes or reaction posts (e.g. post on $2.8T erased: 5 memes in 12 slides; Huang "THIRD AFFAIR": 5 in 11).
- **Neutral, never preachy.** Captions never take sides on politics. Opinion lives only in memes, which mock the situation or "the market", not a group of people.
- **Every story ends at the market.** ~70% of captions end with one sentence: "Traders predict a 41% chance Huang will get divorced before April." That sentence is the product tie-in, and the reason a celebrity scandal belongs on a trading account.

**Topic mix** (189 posts): US politics & Trump ~30%; business/tech/AI ~25% (Nvidia, Musk, OpenAI/Anthropic, Apple); world unrest & geopolitics ~15% (France/Germany/Italy protests, Iran war, Israel, Ceuta); markets & money ~10% (stock crashes, Bitcoin, wealth, paycheck stats); sports (World Cup, Mbappé, NBA) ~10%; culture/society stats ~10% (Gen Z, boomers, alcohol, testosterone, GTA VI).

## 2. Story selection & timing

**What they pick.** A story qualifies when at least two of these are true:
1. A **famous face** people already have an opinion about (Trump, Musk, Jensen Huang, Mbappé).
2. A **shock number** that fits in a headline ($2.8 trillion, $80 billion, 89%, 1.3 trillion).
3. A **"wait, what?" twist** (clean swimmer wins the "Steroid Olympics"; Mbappé "crashed China's economy"; Trump offers UFC's Dana White the press-secretary job).
4. A **stake for the reader**: their money, their generation, their job (4 in 10 Americans earning $300K+ live paycheck to paycheck; boomers want Gen Z to pay more taxes).
5. A **market** that can close the caption.

**Run the saga.** When a story keeps moving, they post every development as its own carousel. Jensen Huang/Nvidia: 12 posts in 5 days (affair, China denial, stock tanks, second affair, board to fire him, resigned, Elon wants to hire him, Tim Cook should take over, testify to Congress, third affair). Three of them passed 44K likes; the account median is 7K. Each follow-up is a full carousel, never a repost, and its headline only says the *new* fact ("SECOND AFFAIR", "RESIGNED", "THIRD AFFAIR").

**Reversals get their own post.** Trump offers Dana White the job (00:03 UTC) → "KIDDING" with an OFFICIAL REPORT tag 2.5 hours later.

**Speed.** Hard news posts the same day, usually within hours ("BREAKING" posts are the freshest). Feature/stat stories (studies, polls) run days or weeks after publication, and they do just as well when the stat is relatable (exhausted adults: 31.1K, 1.4K shares).

**Time of day.** Posts go out between 14:00 and 23:00 UTC (10:00–19:00 New York), several a day. Within that window, hour has no visible effect on likes; the story does.

**What underperforms** (all under 2K likes): an executive most people can't name (Anthropic $80B loss, 1.8K), abstract institutional moves with no hero (Trump threatens Oman, 1.9K; Bolsonaro in Miami, 1.9K), and stories whose photo doesn't match the story (China AI stock crash illustrated by a Trump–Xi handshake, 1.9K).

## 3. Carousel structure

**Length.** Median 12 slides; 76% of posts have 11–14. Short posts (2–8 slides) are follow-ups, quote posts, sports sub-account collabs and simple breaking items. The length is not padding: it comes from alternating information with reaction.

**The spine** (seen in every long carousel):

1. **Hook** — photo, tag, headline.
2. **The fact, restated with detail** — one or two sentences of white text above proof (TV still with chyron, X post, article screenshot, heatmap). Gives the exact figure the hook rounded ("89%" behind a "90%" hook) and the source.
3. **First reaction** — a meme, captioned above ("The market 3 minutes after you click buy").
4. **More proof / context** — second angle: another TV channel, a data board, the person's own words on video.
5. **Reaction.**
6. **The number** — the market chart slide ("Andddd there go the divorce odds."), placed mid-to-late, never first.
7. **One or two more reactions**, the best one last.
8. **End slide** — a fixed promo (Polymarket: "Get $25 to start / code POLYIG" over a phone mockup of a related market). Same design every time; only the code and featured market change.

**By story type:**

| Story type | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Breaking hard news | hook → TV/X proof → who/what/why → reaction memes → market chart → promo (10–12) | "HUANG TO TESTIFY BEFORE CONGRESS…" |
| Market move | price/chart hook → cause (chart + TV) → memes about holders → odds of next level → promo (11–15) | "BTC SURGES ABOVE $80K", "BITCOIN CRASHES TO 21-MONTH LOW" |
| Saga follow-up | one-word hook → the new fact with proof → recap slide (list/grid of everything so far) → memes (8–11) | "THIRD AFFAIR" (recap: "A list of Huang's known romantic relationships" with a 4-portrait grid) |
| Quote | quote-tag hook with the words verbatim → the clip of them saying it → reactions (5–7) | "WHO THE HELL WANTS TO WATCH FOOTBALL, IT'S BORING AS HELL" |
| Reversal | one-word hook + inset of the other party → transcript/X post above the TV clip → context (5–6) | "KIDDING" |
| Stat / study | stat headline over a crowd/scene photo → article screenshot → explainer video → reactions (11–14) | "90% OF BOOMERS WANT GEN Z TO PAY MORE TAXES" |
| Roundup | wide-scene hook → one slide per place/clip (13–15) | "Gen Z-led protests sweeping the world" (29.7K, 1.3K shares) |

**Never repeat information.** Slide 2 never re-says the headline; it adds the precise figure, the source and the "when". Each proof slide shows a different angle (a second channel, a data board, the person on camera), not the same fact twice.

## 4. Slide 1

Reference screenshots: `.news/pm-study/01, 05, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16`.

**Photo.**
- Full-bleed, edge to edge, no frame. The subject's **face sits in the upper half**, usually centred or slightly off-centre, eyes roughly a third of the way down. Head and shoulders fill the width; the bottom 35–40% is under the headline and fade.
- **Emotion over officialdom**: Huang pensive with hand in hair (THIRD AFFAIR), Trump eyes closed and chin up (KIDDING), Trump face-palm (football quote), Mbappé looking down (China). The face must read at thumbnail size in the grid.
- **Two people** when the story is a relationship between them: side by side, both faces above the headline (Elon + Huang; Hegseth + Trump; Kanter + White split screen).
- **No face** when the story is a number or a crowd: red-tinted burning forest for "$2.8 TRILLION ERASED", aerial crowd for "EARTH CAN ONLY SUSTAIN 4B PEOPLE", game screenshot for GTA, the price chart itself for Bitcoin.
- The photo must match the story. The weakest hooks used a generic photo of famous people for a story about something else.

**Round inset.**
- A circle (white ring) of **a second person, a logo, a flag or a data tile**, top-right (sometimes left), in empty space beside the face, never over it.
- Use it to name the *other* party the headline doesn't: Nvidia logo next to Huang ("HUANG TO TESTIFY…"), China flag next to Mbappé, Dana White next to Trump ("KIDDING"), a red/green stock heatmap crop next to Huang ("NVIDIA STOCK TANKS…"), GOP elephant next to Musk.
- One inset at most.

**Tag** (one small pill centred just above the headline, or none):
- **BREAKING** (red) — fresh hard news, same day, something just changed. About 1 in 3 of the 38 recent hooks counted on the grid.
- **TRENDING** (white on black) — the default for stories already circulating, studies, culture, follow-ups to a trend. Just over half of the same 38.
- **OFFICIAL REPORT** — the person/institution confirmed it themselves ("KIDDING").
- **Quote tag** (blue pill with a quote mark) — the headline is the person's words, verbatim.
- No tag on partner posts. Never a topic tag (no "POLITICS", "CRYPTO").

**Headline.**
- Condensed bold sans, all caps, white, centred, as wide as the slide, 1–4 lines. Black gradient behind it, sometimes a halftone dot texture (quote post).
- **One word** when the story is already known and the new fact is the shock: "BREAKING", "RESIGNED", "KIDDING", "THIRD AFFAIR", "SECOND AFFAIR".
- Otherwise **4–10 words, subject + strong verb + number**: "$2.8 TRILLION ERASED IN STOCK MARKET'S WORST DAY EVER", "MUSK SPENT $446M ON MIDTERMS", "TRUMP WANTS ISRAEL TO REPAY $47 BILLION FOR IRAN WAR".
- Verbs they use: erased, tanks, crashes, surges, soar, resigns, probed, denies, refuses, weighs, plans, wants. Present tense.
- Framing that creates a "wait, what?": causal twist ("MBAPPÉ JUST CRASHED CHINA'S ECONOMY"), contrast ("CLEAN SWIMMER WINS FIRST PLACE AT 'STEROID OLYMPICS'"), comparison ("EARTH CAN ONLY SUSTAIN 4B PEOPLE — IT HAS 8.3B RIGHT NOW"), group framing ("BOOMERS" for "Americans over 65").
- They round on the hook (89% → "90%") and correct it on slide 2. **We don't** (see §10).

**Brand mark.** "Polymarket" logo + wordmark, small, top-left, slide 1 only. Inner slides carry no logo.

## 5. Explain & proof slides

- **Text above media** on a white background: one or two short sentences in dark sans, sentence case, at the top; the media below, full width, bottom-anchored or centred. This is the format for every inner slide, facts and memes alike.
- **Text does the telling, media does the proving.** The sentence states the fact with its source and time ("Nvidia's board is reportedly preparing to OUST Jensen Huang as CEO after a second affair was revealed."). Capitals for the one word that matters (OUST, CRASHED, HALVING, STAGGERING, HIGHER). An emoji at the start for breaking items (🚨) or at the end for tone (😂, 🫠) is common on inner slides, not on the hook.
- **Proof types, in order of preference:**
  1. **The person's own words**: video clip of them saying it, or their X/Truth Social post as a screenshot (handle and verification visible).
  2. **TV stills with the channel's chyron and logo** (CNBC, Fox News, Fox Business, CBS, Bloomberg). The chyron itself becomes a second headline ("MARKET SELL-OFF", "AI TRADE RESETS AFTER ELECTION").
  3. **Data screens**: market heatmap (all red), Bloomberg intraday board with red/green tiles, a candlestick chart from a trading app.
  4. **Article/headline screenshots** for studies and reports.
  5. **Their own sub-account's X posts** (Polymarket Sports) as screenshots for breaking sports news.
- **Recap slide** in sagas: a list in plain text plus a grid of round-cornered portraits, with a "?" for the unknown ("Another employee — Unnamed").
- **Memes**: caption above, the meme image or short clip below. The caption carries the joke and ties it to the story: "Jensen watching the news of his third affair break", "POV: You're prepared to hold AI stocks to zero", "Crypto bros checking their portfolio today", "This is not Bitcoin, Ethereum, or a sh*tcoin. It's the Nasdaq having a nightmare." Memes from X are shown as X-post screenshots with the handle.

## 6. Numbers

- **The product chart** is the one number slide every post has: a white card (market icon, question, "41% chance ▲18%", line chart, Polymarket mark) with one casual line above it ("Andddd there go the divorce odds.", "Despite the scare, the AI bubble seems safe for the rest of the year."). It sits in the middle or near the end, after the story has been told.
- **Price stories put the chart on slide 1.** "BTC SURGES ABOVE $80K": asset logo, name, "$80,846.00", "↑ $4,275.19 (+5.58%)" top-left, dark background with a green line rising to the right, tag + headline below. "BITCOIN CRASHES TO 21-MONTH LOW": red candles falling off a cliff on black. Colour carries the direction: green up, red down, nothing else.
- **Market-wide moves use a heatmap** (every tile red) or a broadcaster's board; a single stock's move can be a round inset crop of the heatmap tile on a face photo ("NVIDIA STOCK TANKS…": AAPL +2.29%, NVDA −6.75%).
- **Stat stories** put the stat in the headline and the evidence (article, poll chart) on slide 2; no designed stat tiles.
- The number in the headline is the biggest, simplest version ("$2.8 TRILLION", "$80 BILLION", "1.3 TRILLION"), with the precise figure, source and date on slide 2 and in the caption.

## 7. Captions

- **Structure:** 2–5 short paragraphs, 250–1,100 characters (median ~450). Paragraph 1 is the news in one sentence with the attribution ("Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang reportedly had an affair with another company employee, Reuters reported Thursday, adding to…"). Paragraph 2–3: context, numbers, what is confirmed vs reported. Last line: the market sentence.
- **Market sentence formula:** "Traders predict a/an/only a [X]% chance [specific, dated outcome]." Variants: "There's a 73% chance he testifies by December 31, 2026 according to Polymarket US traders." "The odds of a hantavirus pandemic in 2026 are still sitting low at just 9%." Playful markets are fine when the story is playful ("2% chance Trump will go to space this year", "9% chance Trump renames ICE to NICE"). When no market fits, the caption simply ends on the last fact (~30% of posts).
- **Main editorial voice:** no hashtags, no emojis, no "link in bio", no questions to the audience, no sources list (attribution is inline). Self-censoring of trigger words for reach (c*caine, d*ath).
- **Sub-account voice is different** and shows up on collab posts: sports collabs open with "🚨BREAKING:", use unicode bold (𝐁𝐑𝐄𝐀𝐊𝐈𝐍𝐆), end with "(Via BBC Sport)" and sometimes a question ("Who do you think is going to win the UCL? 👇"). Finance collabs end with "Follow @PolymarketMoney for breaking finance news & updates." plus legal disclaimers. Paid partner posts (@wealth, marked *polymarketpartner) use emojis, ⠀ spacers and 4–5 hashtags, and a different visual template (yellow headline, no tag).
- **Collabs are distribution.** Most posts are Instagram collabs with pages like @polymarketmoney, @insidehistory, @complex, @technology, @daytrading, @dexerto. Many top performers are collabs with large culture pages (Complex: GTA 58.9K; Inside History: swimmer 54.6K, Google AI 34K).

**What it adds up to:**
- **The news decides the ceiling; craft decides whether it is reached.** Every top post has a story with a famous face or a stake for the reader *and* a hook that frames it as a twist. Same-day saga posts with weak framing ("Trump says Tim Cook should take over Nvidia", 2.8K) stay at baseline even inside a hot saga.
- **Money shocks and money anxiety travel.** Market crashes, record numbers, paycheck/tax/generation stats are consistently above p75, and they are the most-shared (1.2K–1.6K shares).
- **Comments come from debate, shares from "you need to see this".** Comment leaders are generational/identity claims (3.1K, 3.9K); share leaders are spectacles and warnings (GTA, $2.8T, AI-agents-in-bank-accounts 420 shares on a 9.9K post).
- **Slide count barely matters.** 11-slide posts median 9.8K, 12–14 slide posts ~5.8–6.9K, 15-slide 10.4K. Length follows the material, it doesn't drive results.
- **Timing within the day doesn't show.** Freshness relative to the event does: breaking posts land within hours, saga follow-ups the same day.

## 9. Never do (what we never saw in ~191 posts)

- A logo or watermark on inner slides; a source credit printed on any slide.
- Two tags, or a topic tag.
- A hook that repeats on slide 2 word for word.
- Hashtags, emojis, CTAs or "link in bio" in a main editorial caption.
- Lowercase or multi-colour headlines on editorial posts (only partner posts use yellow).
- A stock photo or illustration as the hook; an AI image of a real person (the one post Instagram labelled "AI content" did 2.6K).
- Memes about victims: tragedies (plague death, death-penalty case, cartel weapons) run without jokes, fewer slides and no playful odds.
- Taking a political side in the caption.
- More than one market chart slide per post, or the chart before the story is told.

## 10. Adapting to Tradr

Tradr is a social platform for traders, posting as @tradr__app. The audience is male traders, 20–50, who have been burned before and distrust anyone who bends numbers. That changes a few things.

**What replaces the odds.** Polymarket's last caption line and its chart slide come from its own markets. Tradr has no odds. Use, in this order:
1. **The real price move**, from exchange data, with venue and time: "BTC is down 8.4% in 24 hours at $71,240 (Binance, 14:30 UTC)." This is our chart slide and, for price stories, our slide 1 (price card + chart, green up / red down, like "BTC SURGES ABOVE $80K").
2. **Market stats that traders check**: liquidations, volume, funding, the day's biggest movers, gold/oil/indices levels, the S&P/Nasdaq day — each with its source.
3. **Tradr's own aggregate trade data** (e.g. how many Tradr traders opened BTC longs today), only when it is real platform data and Mohamed has approved using it.
Never a forecast or a price target. Nothing on Tradr is advice.

**Story selection for Tradr.** Same filter (§2), with one extra test: *does it move a market a trader can trade?* Crypto, forex, gold, indices, big single stocks, central banks, and the famous people who move them. Polymarket's best-performing finance shapes map directly: record-size moves ("$2.8 TRILLION ERASED"), price milestones ("BTC SURGES ABOVE $80K"), a face behind a stock move ("NVIDIA STOCK TANKS AFTER…"), and money-anxiety stats. Run sagas as follow-ups when a market story keeps moving.

**Numbers are never rounded in our favour or anyone's.** Polymarket's "90% of boomers" hook for an 89% poll is a pattern we don't copy: Tradr's whole promise is that nobody can lie on it. Use the exact figure, or an honest rounding that the source supports ("nearly 9 in 10").

**Brand.** Our logo small, top-left, **slide 1 only**, like Polymarket's mark. Brand green **#00C16A** for up-moves, the quote tag and accents; red only for down-moves. The fixed end slide (in Studio) replaces Polymarket's promo slide.

**Workflow.** Every post is drafted in Tradr Studio, reviewed by Mohamed and posted by hand. Speed therefore comes from preparation (templates, sources ready), not from auto-posting.

**Decisions for Mohamed** (the evidence differs from `docs/news-style-guide.md`):
- **Memes.** Decided 13 Nov: allowed on inside slides (§11).
- **Captions.** Polymarket's main account uses no hashtags and no sources list (attribution inline). The style guide asks for a "Sources:" line and 3–6 hashtags.
- **🚨 and tags.** Polymarket uses 🚨 only in sports sub-account captions; its BREAKING / TRENDING / OFFICIAL REPORT / quote tags match the style guide.
- **Gossip.** The Huang affair saga was the account's biggest run of the period (3 posts above 44K). The style guide rules out gossip; a market angle (Nvidia shares) is the bridge if Mohamed wants such stories.
- **Collabs.** Decided 13 Nov: one daily collab with @range.fx (§11).

## 11. Tradr rules added 13 Nov 2026 (Mohamed)

**Memes — allowed on inside slides, never on slide 1.** Used the way Polymarket uses them (§1, §5): a reaction slide placed after a fact slide, with our one-line caption above and the meme below. The caption carries the joke and ties it to the story ("Apple when someone asks about its AI data-centre budget"). Rules:
- At most ~1 meme for every 2–3 fact slides; the best one goes last, before the end slide.
- Mock the situation or "the market", never a group of people, never a victim, never a trader by name. No memes on tragedies.
- Real meme images only: known templates (imgflip's blank library works when the image needs no words), or a real meme/reaction post shown as an X screenshot with its handle. Never an AI-made image of a real person.
- Picked by the editor; no need to ask.
- _13 Nov: first used — Monkey Puppet on Apple's record, Surprised Pikachu on Anthropic's IPO._

**The daily collab — @range.fx.** Every day, the single strongest news post is the range.fx collab: Mohamed adds **@range.fx** as collaborator when he posts it. One per day only. Strongest = the post with the highest ceiling by §2 and §8 (famous face × shock number × twist, a saga at its peak, a record money move). Mark it with `desk collab <draftId> <YYYY-MM-DD>` (it clears any other post marked for that day); News shows a green **Collab** pill and a note on that post.
- _13 Nov: Nvidia loses almost $500B (saga peak, famous face, record-size number)._

**Times on screenshots are US Eastern.** Every screenshot with a timestamp (Truth Social, X, articles, live blogs) shows US Eastern time (ET), the time most readers and the outlets use. `shot.ts` renders in America/New_York. Truth Social in Chrome shows the browser's time zone: before capturing, set the post's time to its `created_at` in ET (the same instant, written the way an Eastern reader sees it). If a screenshot can't be taken in ET, don't use it until it can.
- _13 Nov: the two Truth Social screenshots already in News (Iran post, Tim Cook post) still show CET; Truth Social put up a "Verify you are human" check, which we never click. Re-capture when it lets the page through._

## How to update

Each news run adds what it learns here: a dated line under the section it concerns (what we tried, the numbers after 48 hours, what we'd keep). When a rule changes, edit it in place and note the date. Restudy @polymarket (or another reference account) every couple of months, append to `.news/pm-study/log.md`, and refresh §8's table and baseline.
